Dashcam footage, replayed as a clay model of the street.

Give it a recorded clip and it plays the video beside a matte 3D scene of the same drive: every car, truck, bus, bike and person placed on the road around you, on a street rebuilt from the footage.

View on GitHub Run it locally

Python and Three.js. Runs on your own machine. A personal toy, not a driver aid.

What you get

The clip and the clay scene play in step. Scrub anywhere, step a frame at a time or slow it down.

Chase, aerial and top views

Chase follows your car, aerial looks down at an angle and top is a plan view. Press 1, 2 or 3, or drag to orbit.

The street, rebuilt

Road edges, lane paint, arrows, crosswalks, trees, poles and buildings, read from the footage and laid along the path your car took.

A debug view that shows its working

Press D for boxes and distances on the video, the horizon and hood lines, and the camera calibration it worked out.

Night and fog

It runs on night footage too. Speed is read from road texture, so it gets less reliable when the road is hard to see.

How it works

A Python pipeline turns a clip into one scene file, and a static viewer plays that file beside the video. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

  1. 01

    Detect and track

    YOLO finds cars, trucks, buses, motorcycles, bicycles and people in each frame. ByteTrack links them over time and a smoother cleans up each track.

  2. 02

    Calibrate from the footage

    With no camera specs to go on, it finds the horizon from how the road flows, the camera height from the widths of cars ahead, and where the hood starts. Each shot gets its own calibration, so compilations work too.

  3. 03

    Estimate your own motion

    Speed and turning come from the road texture moving past, so other traffic can be placed on the road rather than relative to a moving camera.

  4. 04

    Rebuild the street

    A street segmentation model labels road, paint, curbs, trees, poles and buildings. They are projected onto a flat road and joined along your path.

  5. 05

    Replay it

    Everything lands in a JSON scene file. The Three.js viewer plays it beside the video, from plain static files with no backend.

Limits

It's a toy, and it is guessing a lot from one camera. Worth knowing before you read too much into it.

  • Not a driver aid

    Nothing here drives, warns or should be trusted for anything safety-related.

  • Distances have an unknown scale

    On a clip from a camera you don't know, the field of view is a guess. The layout holds up; absolute metres can be off by 20% or more.

  • Speed is unreliable on fast roads and wide lenses

    Speed is read from the road moving past. At motorway speed it currently collapses to near zero, and wide dashcam lenses read it low. City driving works best.

  • One forward camera

    Nothing is known behind or beside you. Cars that pass you coast briefly and fade, and the street is an estimate, not a survey.

Run it locally

You need uv, bun and ffmpeg. The first run downloads about 1 GB of models into models/.

  1. 01

    Install the pipeline

    With mise, mise trust && mise install gets the pinned versions.

    git clone https://github.com/snehanchakravarthi/clayview
cd clayview
uv sync
  2. 02

    Process a clip

    Put a clip in clips/. H.264 MP4 is the safe choice. This writes out/drive.scene.json.

    uv run clayview process clips/drive.mp4
  3. 03

    Open the viewer

    Go to localhost:5199 and press Open latest.

    cd viewer
bun install
bun run dev

Got an .ogv or something else your browser can't play? Convert it first:

ffmpeg -i clip.ogv -c:v libx264 -crf 23 -an clip.mp4
