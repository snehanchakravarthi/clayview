    Computer Link

    Compute

    • Raspberry Pi 5 with the 26 TOPS AI HAT+ is the main brain. It has four USB ports, two of them USB 3, so four cameras plug in directly and six need a powered hub.
    • Budget is about 135 detections a second at 640 px: Hailo's 270 fps vendor figure, halved by my estimate for a real pipeline. Divide by the number of cameras.
    • The HAT uses the Pi's one PCIe slot, so storage stays on the SD card unless you add a splitter. Mount the system read-only so power cuts can't corrupt it.

    Power and heat

    • Take power from a switched fuse with a fuse tap, so the Pi stops when the ignition does and can't drain the battery.
    • Use a 12 V to 5 V, 5 A converter. On a smaller supply the Pi 5 limits USB devices to 600 mA in total, which four cameras can exceed. Without USB-PD, set usb_max_current_enable=1.
    • Heat is the real enemy. A parked car in the sun gets far hotter than the Pi likes. Keep it low and shaded, with the active cooler fitted.
    • Add a small backup supply so it can shut down cleanly after the ignition goes off.

    How the numbers are made

    This is geometry, not a test on a real car. Each camera is reduced to a lens angle, a height, and a tilt. Seen means the object is in frame, clear of your own bodywork, and at least 16 detector pixels wide. Placed means the estimated error is under 15% of the distance, or 0.5 m when close.

    Distance uses the best of three methods: tyre contact on a flat road, the object's apparent size, or two cameras crossing their sightlines. A car cut off by the frame loses the size and two-camera methods, which is why cars beside the doors are hard.

    • Likely too harsh: no tracking over time, which should smooth errors. No learned depth model, which is what makes Tesla's own layout work, so its score here is not a verdict on Tesla.
    • Likely too kind: fisheye edges are stretched and YOLO is weaker there, so those frames may need splitting into flat views, which costs compute. Rain, night and dirty lenses are ignored.
